# For Students 
## 🎯 How  To Use These Examples

- **Focus on Theory:**  Prioritize understanding concepts and exploring interdisciplinary connections between AI and health/sport sciences.
- **Document the Process:**  Document literature reviews and theoretical frameworks in a structured way (e.g., summaries, flowcharts, presentations).
- **Highlight Insights:**  Emphasize your insights into how AI can address challenges in health/sport sciences, as this demonstrates critical thinking and interdisciplinary knowledge.

## 💡 Additional Tips 

- **Start Small:** Begin with 2-3 selected papers per week to avoid overwhelm.
- **Take Notes:** Use a structured template (e.g., Cornell notes) to summarize papers and identify key takeaways.
- **Connect the Dots:** Focus on how AI techniques (e.g., machine learning, computer vision) can solve specific problems in health/sport sciences.
- **Communicate Challenges:** If a concept is unclear even after discussing it with your favorite LLM, mention it in the report so supervisors can provide guidance.


# For Supervisors
## 🎯 How  To Use These Examples

- **Always Provide Feedback:** Encourage students to reflect on their learning, challenges, and insights. Provide constructive feedback to help them improve their understanding and reporting skills.
- **Encourage Critical Thinking:** Ask students to connect their literature reviews and theoretical frameworks to real-world applications in health/sport sciences, fostering critical thinking and interdisciplinary knowledge.    


## 💡 Additional Tips
- **Set Clear Expectations:** Clearly communicate the objectives and expectations for weekly reports, including the importance of documenting literature reviews, theoretical frameworks, and insights.
- **Encourage Reflection:** Prompt students to reflect on their learning process, challenges faced, and how they overcame them. This helps develop critical thinking and problem-solving skills.
- **Show How You Do It:** Share relevant papers, articles, or tools and explain (during a meeting) how you found them and why they are relevant to the student's project. This helps students learn (by observing your approach) how to conduct effective literature reviews and identify valuable resources.
